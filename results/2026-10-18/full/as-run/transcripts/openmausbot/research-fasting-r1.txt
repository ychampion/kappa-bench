reply:
**Short answer:** No. When total calories are matched, intermittent fasting (IF) works about as well as ordinary calorie restriction. It isn't clearly better. It helps mainly if the schedule makes it easier for you to eat less and stick with it.

**What the evidence shows**

- **Head-to-head trials find no real difference in weight loss.** A 1-year RCT compared alternate-day fasting with daily calorie restriction. Weight loss was similar, and the fasting group had more dropouts and no better cardiometabolic markers (Trepanowski et al., *JAMA Internal Medicine*, 2017).
- **Time-restricted eating (for example 16:8) adds little beyond cutting calories.**
  - A 12-week RCT of 16:8 found no significant weight-loss advantage over regular meal timing. Some of the weight lost was lean mass (Lowe et al., *JAMA Internal Medicine*, 2020).
  - A 12-month RCT in people with obesity compared an 8-hour eating window plus calorie restriction against calorie restriction alone. It found no added benefit from the window (Liu et al., *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2022).
- **Pooled analyses agree.** Reviews of many trials find IF produces modest weight loss, typically a few percent of body weight. That is similar to continuous restriction, with comparable effects on blood pressure, glucose and lipids (for example, Patikorn et al., *JAMA Network Open*, 2021, an umbrella review of meta-analyses).

**Caveats**
- Most trials are short (months) and fairly small, and long-term data are limited.
- Adherence matters more than the pattern. Some people find IF simpler, and others find it harder.
- IF is not suitable for everyone, including pregnancy, a history of eating disorders, and people on insulin or sulfonylureas. They should check with a clinician first.
- Some earlier hints of metabolic benefits independent of weight loss haven't held up consistently in the larger trials.

**Sources**
1. Trepanowski JF et al. *JAMA Intern Med.* 2017;177(7):930–938.
2. Lowe DA et al. *JAMA Intern Med.* 2020;180(11):1491–1499.
3. Liu D et al. *N Engl J Med.* 2022;386:1495–1504.
4. Patikorn C et al. *JAMA Netw Open.* 2021;4(12):e2139558.
