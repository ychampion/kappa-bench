reply:
**Short answer:** No. Intermittent fasting (IF) works for weight loss, but the evidence says it does about as well as ordinary calorie restriction, not better. I read all four sources on 18 Oct 2026.

- **Newest and strongest evidence (Cochrane review, Feb 2026):** It pooled 22 randomized trials with 1,995 adults who were overweight or obese. Against regular dietary advice (21 studies), IF's weight loss differed by only −0.33% (95% CI −0.92 to 0.26). It also wasn't more likely to get people to a 5% loss (RR 0.98). Against doing nothing, IF did better (−3.42%). The authors judged that gap unlikely to be clearly clinically meaningful. Certainty was low for the advice comparison, and most trials ran only 6–12 months ([News-Medical summary of the Cochrane review](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260217/Does-intermittent-fasting-help-you-lose-more-weight-Evidence-review-finds-no-clear-advantage.aspx)).
- **Earlier meta-analysis (11 trials, 2018):** It compared 5:2 and alternate-day styles with continuous restriction over 8–24 weeks. The pooled difference was −0.61 kg (95% CI −1.70 to 0.47), which isn't significant. Blood sugar, lipids and blood pressure didn't differ either. Fasting insulin was slightly lower with IF, but the authors called that clinically uncertain. Dropouts were often higher in the IF arms, and some participants reported more hunger ([Cioffi et al., J Transl Med](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6304782/)).
- **Longer trials (6+ months):** Across 9 studies, neither approach came out ahead on weight loss. The authors flagged that long-term data are scarce and the trials small ([Headland et al., Nutrients 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4924195/)).

**Where experts disagree:** Reacting to the Cochrane review, some experts said it shows IF offers little benefit. Others argued it lumps very different regimens together and that the "doing nothing" groups often lose weight too. Both camps still agree IF isn't clearly worse than calorie restriction. Prof. Leonie Heilbronn said both typically give about 7% weight loss over 6–12 months with support, and that weight regain after a year is common whichever you use ([Science Media Centre](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-cochrane-review-looking-at-intermittent-fasting-and-weight-loss/)).

**What I'd act on:** The practical takeaway is to pick whichever approach you can stick to. IF has no proven weight-loss edge. The evidence is mostly short-term, and I couldn't find solid long-term data in what I read.
