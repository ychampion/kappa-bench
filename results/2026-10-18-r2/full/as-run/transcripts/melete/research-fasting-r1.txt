reply:
**Intermittent fasting works for weight loss, but the evidence says it isn't better than ordinary calorie restriction.** Most trials find about the same loss either way, so the best choice is probably whichever one you can stick to.

- **Newest review (Cochrane, Feb 2026):** It pooled 22 trials with 1,995 adults with overweight or obesity. Against regular dietary advice, which mostly meant daily calorie cutting, fasting gave a mean difference of −0.33% body weight (95% CI −0.92 to 0.26). That is statistically no difference, and the certainty was rated low. I read this through [News-Medical's summary](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260217/Does-intermittent-fasting-help-you-lose-more-weight-Evidence-review-finds-no-clear-advantage.aspx), not the Cochrane page itself.
- **Earlier meta-analysis (2018):** [Cioffi et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6304782/) pooled 11 trials of 8–24 weeks and found comparable weight loss (−0.61 kg, 95% CI −1.70 to 0.47). Fasting dropouts were often higher, and some trials reported more hunger.
- **Longest single trial (12 months, 139 adults):** Time-restricted eating (an 8-hour eating window) plus calorie restriction lost 8.0 kg, against 6.3 kg for calorie restriction alone. The difference of −1.8 kg (95% CI −4.0 to 0.4) was not significant. I read this through the [authors' own commentary on the trial](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11749637/).
- **Experts disagree on how to read the Cochrane result:** Statistician Baptiste Leurent said the evidence shows "little benefit". Nutrition researcher Adam Collins said fasting is still an effective tool, and that "regular dietary advice" in these trials is itself a calorie deficit. Leonie Heilbronn said the review lumps together fasting types that differ, such as alternate-day fasting and time-restricted eating. Her view is that both fasting and calorie restriction give roughly 7% weight loss at 6–12 months. These are from the [Science Media Centre roundup](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-cochrane-review-looking-at-intermittent-fasting-and-weight-loss/).

**Gaps:**
- Most trials last 6–12 months, so long-term maintenance is unclear.
- Evidence on quality of life and side effects is thin.
- Some small trials hint at benefits beyond weight, but I didn't check those.

I'd act on the Cochrane finding and the 12-month trial, since they agree and are the strongest evidence. All of this is general evidence, not medical advice, so check with a clinician if you have diabetes or take medication.
